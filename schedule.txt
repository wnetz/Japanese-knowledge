September 2026 — solidify N5

Main goal: make the fundamentals dependable.

Keep moving through your textbook, finish/approach finishing Bunpro N5, and continue the random grammar exercises we've been doing.

I'd particularly attack the errors we're currently finding:

particles → conjugations → selecting the appropriate grammar construction.

Not because those are the only things you need, but because they're currently preventing grammar you do know from coming out reliably.

October 2026 — transition firmly into N4

Start emphasizing mixed review.

I shouldn't say:

Use 〜たらどうですか.

I should simply say:

Why don't you ask your teacher?

You decide what Japanese construction expresses it.

Reading increases slightly.

November 2026 — N4

Continue Bunpro at ~14/week.

Start giving yourself longer Japanese passages instead of isolated sentences. You don't need to understand every word.

Start trying to understand:

Who? What happened? Why? What is the speaker saying?

without translating every sentence.

December 2026 — N4 consolidation

I'd make December partly a checkpoint month.

By the end of the year, I want things we're currently practicing—なら、たら、ば、ことがある、ほうがいい、ことができる、なる, etc.—to feel mundane.

Not necessarily flawless production, but recognition should be immediate.

January–March 2027 — N3 transition

This is when the balance starts changing.

Less time doing isolated beginner exercises.

More:

reading + listening + grammar in context.

Keep Bunpro moving at 14/week and SRS running, but start consuming substantially more Japanese.

By March I'd want you tackling N3 reading material without feeling completely overwhelmed.

April–June 2027 — N3 → N2 transition

This is probably the most important phase.

By now, beginner Japanese should require very little deliberate thought.

Weekend reading becomes 60+ minutes.

We also start doing JLPT-style comprehension occasionally—not necessarily full tests yet.

By June, I'd like you to take an N3 practice test.

If you're comfortably passing N3 at this point:

🟢 We're in very good shape for N2.

If you're barely passing:

🟡 Adjust the second half of the year.

If N3 is still extremely difficult:

🔴 We identify why before piling on N2.

July–September 2027 — N2 push

This is where Japanese becomes increasingly input-driven.

Grammar study continues, but reading/listening become the largest components.

Weekends might eventually look like:

60–90m reading
45–60m listening
SRS
grammar review

rather than hours of textbook exercises.

By September, ideally you've encountered essentially all the Bunpro N2 material at least once.

October 2027 — N2 consolidation

Stop caring about finishing decks.

Start caring about whether you can answer N2 questions.

Take your first complete timed N2 practice test.

That gives us:

Vocabulary score
Grammar/reading score
Listening score
Timing problems

Then study based on the results.

November 2027 — exam mode

Several timed practice sections each week.

At least 2–3 full N2 simulations during the month, spaced apart.

SRS continues, but new cards drop dramatically.

No giant last-minute vocabulary dump.

December 2027 — JLPT

Very little new material immediately before the test.

Maintain what you know, practice reading/listening, sleep properly, and take the exam.
